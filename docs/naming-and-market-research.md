Research brief for the company entity behind **DefectFlow**.
Date: 2026-08-01.

## 1. Verdict on the two candidate names

### ❌ "Visibility Construction Technologies" — don't

| Conflict | Who | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Visibuild** | Melbourne, founded 2021, **$6.6M** from Skip Capital. Construction **quality & defect management** — defect tracking, ITPs, inspections. 1,800+ companies, 700+ projects. | This is your **most direct competitor** *and* it already owns the "visi-" territory in Australian construction quality software. Naming yourself "Visibility…" in the same niche is the worst case: confusingly similar, in the same class, to a funded incumbent. |
| **Visibility Corporation** | US ERP vendor with an explicit **Engineering & Construction** vertical. | Same industry, same software category — a real trademark obstacle. |
| **Visible Construction Corp** | US commercial general contractor (FL/NY/TX). | Lower risk (services, not software), but adds noise. |

Beyond the conflicts: it's 33 characters, awkward to say, awkward to type, and
"visibility" is **descriptive** — descriptive marks are the hardest to register
and the weakest to enforce. You'd spend years building equity in a word you can
never own.

### ❌ "Hi Vis Technologies" — don't, and this one is worse

The pun is genuinely good. Every Australian on a site wears hi-vis. But:

| Conflict | Who | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Hi-Vis®** | An **existing construction SaaS product** — cloud-based scaffold management, `hi-visapp.com`, from Global Site Solutions, distributed with **Doka**. Uses the ® symbol. | Direct hit. Same industry, same category (construction SaaS), asserting registered rights. This alone is disqualifying. |
| **Hi-Vis Group** | Australian, family-owned **since 1960**. ~50+ staff across Newcastle (HQ), Sydney, QLD. Signage & safety into construction and mining. Brands itself "**H**ighly **I**ntelligent **V**isual **I**nformation **S**ystems". | Decades of Australian common-law reputation in construction, and they're already reaching toward "systems"/tech language. |
| **Generic use** | Hi Vis Workwear Australia, HIVIS signage, and dozens more. | "Hi-vis" is everyday descriptive vocabulary for high-visibility clothing. Crowded and near-unregistrable. |

**The trap:** a name that is instantly understood is usually a name nobody can
own. "Hi Vis" is maximum recognition, zero defensibility — and it's already
taken by a construction software product.

## 2. What you actually need to know about Australian name law

The single most expensive misunderstanding in Australian startup naming:

> **An ASIC company name is not a trademark, and the two registers do not
> talk to each other.**

- **ASIC** will register your company name unless it is *identical or nearly
  identical* to one already on **its own** register. It does **not** check
  IP Australia.
- So you can be validly registered at ASIC and still be infringing someone's
  trademark — and be forced to rebrand after you've printed everything.
- **Only a registered trademark** gives exclusive rights to the brand.

**Classes you need** for this product:

| Class | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Class 9** | Downloadable software / mobile apps |
| **Class 42** | SaaS — software as a service |

Register both. Class 9 alone doesn't protect a hosted product; Class 42 alone
doesn't protect the App Store build you have on the roadmap (Phase 5).

**Domains:** `.com.au` and direct `.au` require an **Australian presence** —
in practice an ABN — so register the entity first, then the domains.

## 3. The Australian market — this is a good market to be in

### The customer base is overwhelmingly your target

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Registered construction businesses (ABS, June 2025) | **452,820** |
| Construction entities that are small (<20 employees) | **98.3%** of ~462,900 |
| Growth in construction software adoption, 2025 YoY | **+34%**, *driven by firms under 10 employees* |

That last row is the whole thesis. The fastest-growing segment of Australian
construction software buyers is *sub-10-employee builders* — which is exactly
the "independent supervisor / small builder" persona in the DefectFlow
monetization plan.

### The regulatory tailwind is strong and getting stronger

- **Building Commission NSW, Jan–Nov 2026:** 32 rectification orders to
  developers of apartment buildings, plus **237 rectification orders to
  builders of smaller projects** (standalone homes). ~45% were complied with
  or revoked; enforcement action was under consideration against 30 developers
  for non-compliance.
- **NSW Fair Trading and Building Legislation Amendment Bill 2026** — a
  structural response to defective construction and developer insolvency,
  including **Decennial Liability Insurance**.

**Why this matters commercially:** defect documentation is shifting from *good
practice* to *legal and insurance evidence*. The pitch stops being "stay
organised" and becomes **"prove you rectified it, with a timestamped photo
trail, when the regulator or insurer asks."** That is a much easier sale, and
it makes the paid tier's *"photos kept indefinitely"* the actual product —
free-tier photo expiry is then obviously the right lever.

### Competitive position — your pricing is the wedge

| Product | Positioning | Indicative price |
|---|---|---|
| **PlanRadar** | The defect/snagging leader | from **~€26/user/mo**; USD tiers quoted ~$49 / $159 / $239 |
| **Visibuild** | AU, quality/defects/ITPs, enterprise builders | enterprise |
| **Procore / Buildertrend** | Full construction OS | enterprise |
| **Buildxact** | AU (Melbourne), residential takeoff/estimating, raised $18.5M | mid |
| **DefectFlow** | Defects only, mobile-first | **$3.49/user/mo** |

You are roughly **an order of magnitude cheaper than PlanRadar**. That is a
real, defensible wedge — PlanRadar's price structurally ignores the sole trader
and the three-person builder, and that's 98.3% of the market. But it dictates
the brand: you need a name that reads *simple and cheap-to-start*, not
*enterprise-corporate*. "Visibility Construction Technologies Pty Ltd" is
priced like $239/seat. It is not a $3.49 name.

## 4. Recommendation

**Name the company after the product: `DefectFlow Pty Ltd`.**

Reasons:

1. **The field looks clean.** Extensive searching surfaced **no** existing
   "DefectFlow" — no company, product, or meaningful web presence. That is
   rare and worth a lot. (Still needs the three official checks.)
2. **It's registrable.** "Defect" alone is descriptive and weak, but
   *DefectFlow* is a coined compound — the kind of mark IP Australia will
   actually grant in classes 9 and 42.
3. **You've already built the assets.** Icons, manifest, service worker, the
   Supabase project, the README — all already DefectFlow.
4. **One product, one brand.** A pre-revenue single-product SaaS should never
   fund two brands out of one customer's attention.

**The one honest argument against it:** "defect" is a negative word, and the
roadmap (Phase 3 job/site management, manager dashboards) expands past defects.
If DefectFlow becomes a general site-management tool, the name gets tight.

**How to keep both options open:** incorporate a broader parent —
e.g. `Stringline Technologies Pty Ltd` — and trade as **DefectFlow**. Costs one
extra business-name registration and keeps the door open. If you'd rather stay
simple: `DefectFlow Pty Ltd`, and worry about it if you're lucky enough to
outgrow it.

A sharp wedge name beats a vague broad name at your stage. Sell the wedge.

## 5. The heap of options

Sorted by how ownable they are. ⚠️ = likely crowded, check hard.

### Tier 1 — trade vernacular, distinctive, most defensible

These come from the language of the site, which makes them credible to builders
while still being arbitrary enough to trademark.

| Name | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Stringline** | The line pulled taut so a build runs true. Perfect metaphor for accuracy, unmistakably trade. Great parent-company name. |
| **Plumb** / **Plumbline** | "Plumb" = true, upright, correct. Short, punchy, memorable. |
| **Datum** | The surveying reference point everything is measured from. Clean, techy, short domain. |
| **Setout** | The setting-out of a build — the first thing you must get right. |
| **Makegood** | Australian vernacular for *rectifying defects*. Literally describes the product, in the customer's own words. Strong. |
| **Closeout** | The end-of-project phase where defects get cleared. |
| **Squareaway** / **Square** ⚠️ | "Square it away." (Square the payments company makes this hard.) |
| **Trueline** | Building "true" — accurate. |
| **Chalkline** | Same family as Stringline, slightly softer. |
| **Lintel**, **Purlin**, **Batten**, **Joist**, **Trestle**, **Gantry** | Building components — obscure enough to own outright, concrete enough to feel like construction. **Lintel** and **Joist** are the best of these. |

### Tier 2 — category words: instantly clear, weaker trademark

| Name | Note |
|---|---|
| **DefectFlow** | Current. Recommended. |
| **Snaglist** / **Snagline** / **Snagpoint** ⚠️ | "Snagging" is the category term — but PlanRadar dominates that SEO. |
| **Handover** ⚠️ | The moment defects turn into money. Evocative but generic. |
| **Signoff** ⚠️ | The completion moment. Generic. |
| **Rectify** ⚠️ | Literally the word in NSW *rectification orders*. Great resonance, likely taken. |

| **DefectDesk**, **SnagDesk**, **FixList**, **PunchPoint**, **WorksLog**, **SiteLedger** | Clear compounds. Safe, unexciting, easy to clear. |

### Tier 3 — avoid

**Visibility Construction Technologies**, **Hi Vis Technologies**, **HiVis

## Before you register

Run these three checks in this order, on your shortlist, before spending a
dollar. All three, every time — passing one proves nothing about the others.

1. **ASIC company/business name** — <https://asic.gov.au/> (ASIC Connect →
   "Check name availability"). Free.
2. **IP Australia trademark** — <https://search.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademarks/search/quick>.
   Search your name **and** near-misses, filtered to **classes 9 and 42**.
   Free. This is the one that can force a rebrand later — do not skip it.
   Consider **TM Headstart** for a pre-assessment before filing.
3. **Domains** — `.com.au` and `.au` via any auDA registrar; `.com` via any
   registrar. Requires an ABN for the `.au` namespaces, so incorporate first.

Ballpark costs (**indexed annually — verify current figures**): ASIC company
registration ~$600; business name ~$45/yr or ~$105/3yrs; trademark roughly
$250–$400 **per class** (so double it for 9 + 42).

Worth paying a trademark attorney for one hour on the final shortlist. It is
vastly cheaper than rebranding a product that has customers.
